# Scope boundaries: what the app is NOT, and who we optimise for

The explicit no-s are as load-bearing as the yes-s — they stop the product drifting back into the
"several ideas" it evolved from. The app is deliberately **not** the following four things:

1. **Not a proposal-writing / application-submission tool.** It helps you *decide* whether and where
   to apply; it does not draft, manage, or submit the bid. It points to the official EU Funding &
   Tenders portal and never impersonates or replaces it. (Direct corollary of the core job: "decide
   where to spend scarce effort," not "do the work.")
2. **Not a research-paper / citation / publications graph.** It is about *funding*, not scholarship —
   no papers, authors, citations, or h-indexes. This is the single most important creep-guard:
   because the project is internally codenamed "knowledge graph," bolting on OpenAlex/Scholar-style
   bibliometrics is the most tempting wrong turn. Nailed shut.
3. **Not a live grants-alert CRM.** The app *may* remember what you flagged (a lightweight
   shortlist/watch — the retention hook) and *may* surface "what changed" in your fields, but it does
   **not** manage applications as records: no submission-status tracking, no per-lead pipeline stages,
   no external email/deadline reminders, no "assigned-to" workflow. Monitoring + shortlist = yes;
   pipeline / status / reminders = no. This keeps the retention engine while avoiding both a scope
   balloon and a head-to-head with institutional research-management systems (a different, crowded
   market) instead of our evidence wedge.
4. **Not an adjudicator.** It surfaces, ranks by grounded relevance, and cites the evidence, but never
   issues a verdict: no eligibility ruling ("you qualify" — that is a formal Horizon determination the
   app cannot stand behind; show the criteria, don't rule), no winnability score ("73% fit"), no
   single "this is the one to pick." Ranking ≠ verdict. This concerns *verdicts, not retrieval*: the
   grounded assistant may recommend and cite matching calls (see the grounded-assistant scope decision,
   forthcoming from Q4.3) — the boundary is that it stops short of ruling. An "intelligence" product is
   under constant pressure to start advising; this NOT protects that flank.

**Who we optimise for.** The primary user is the **research-office professional** (grant advisor /
research manager) at a university or research organisation, with the ambitious **PI** they support as
a served, secondary user. We optimise the product — especially the home's default lens — for the
advisor's portfolio/fields mental model, because (a) the advisor's daily job *is* the deliberation
half that is our differentiator, protecting the "intelligence" positioning; (b) their field/portfolio
view scales down to the PI's single-topic view as a filter, whereas the reverse does not scale up; and
(c) they have a recurring, job-driven reason to monitor, which is the retention engine. The genuine
trade-off accepted: the PI is the larger, more viral market (the "burned a season" pain is theirs),
so this is a bet on depth-of-fit over breadth-of-adoption. Recorded because a future reader will
reasonably ask "why not optimise for the far more numerous PIs?"
